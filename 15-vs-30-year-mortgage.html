15-Year vs 30-Year Mortgage: Which One is Right for You?

Published on: October 25, 2026 | Written by: MortgageCalc Team

When you apply for a mortgage, one of the most important decisions you will make is choosing the loan term. The two most common options are a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage and a 15-year fixed-rate mortgage.

Both have distinct advantages and disadvantages depending on your financial situation, monthly budget, and long-term goals. Choosing the wrong term can either strain your monthly cash flow or cost you tens of thousands of dollars in extra interest over time. This guide breaks down the differences so you can make an informed decision.

The 30-Year Mortgage: Lower Payments, Higher Interest

A 30-year mortgage is the most popular choice for homebuyers, especially first-time buyers. It spreads the repayment of the loan over 360 months.

Pros of a 30-Year Mortgage:

  • Lower Monthly Payments: Because the loan is stretched over a longer period, your monthly principal and interest payment is significantly lower. This gives you more breathing room in your monthly budget.
  • Affordability: It allows you to qualify for and buy a more expensive home than you could with a 15-year term.
  • Inflation Benefit: You are paying back the loan with future dollars, which are generally worth less due to inflation.

Cons of a 30-Year Mortgage:

  • Massive Interest: You will pay a much higher amount of interest over the life of the loan.
  • Slower Equity Building: You build home equity much slower in the early years because most of your early payments go toward interest.

The 15-Year Mortgage: Higher Payments, Massive Savings

A 15-year mortgage is ideal for buyers who can afford higher monthly payments and want to be debt-free faster. It spreads the repayment over 180 months.

Pros of a 15-Year Mortgage:

  • Huge Interest Savings: You will pay significantly less interest over the life of the loan. Often tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars less.
  • Lower Interest Rates: Lenders usually offer a slightly lower interest rate for 15-year loans compared to 30-year loans because the risk is lower for them.
  • Faster Equity: You build equity in your home much faster, giving you more options for refinancing or home equity loans later.

Cons of a 15-Year Mortgage:

  • Higher Monthly Payments: Your monthly payment will be much higher, which can put a strain on your budget.
  • Less Flexibility: If you face a financial emergency or job loss, the higher mandatory payment can be difficult to manage.

Which One Should You Choose?

There is no one-size-fits-all answer. Here is a simple rule of thumb:

  • Choose a 30-year mortgage if: You are a first-time buyer, your budget is tight, you want to invest your extra cash elsewhere, or you plan to move or refinance within 10 years.
  • Choose a 15-year mortgage if: You have a high, stable income, you want to be debt-free before retirement, and you can comfortably afford the higher monthly payment without being "house poor."
Pro Tip: You can always take a 30-year mortgage but make extra payments (like paying an extra $100 a month or making biweekly payments). This gives you the safety of a lower mandatory payment but allows you to pay it off early and save on interest.
