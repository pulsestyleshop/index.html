How Much House Can I Afford? (The 28/36 Rule)

Published on: October 27, 2026 | Written by: MortgageCalc Team

Before you start browsing real estate listings or attending open houses, you need to answer one crucial question: How much house can I actually afford?

It is easy to fall in love with a beautiful home, but if the monthly payments stretch your budget too thin, it can lead to financial stress. Lenders use specific formulas to determine how much they are willing to lend you, but ultimately, you need to decide what fits comfortably within your lifestyle.

In this guide, we will break down the standard rules lenders use, how to calculate your budget, and the hidden costs you must consider.

The 28/36 Rule: The Standard Lender Guideline

Most financial experts and lenders use a simple formula known as the **28/36 Rule** to determine mortgage affordability. Here is how it works:

1. The Front-End Ratio (28%)

Your total monthly housing expenses should not exceed **28% of your gross (pre-tax) monthly income**. This includes:

  • Principal and Interest (P&I)
  • Property Taxes
  • Homeowners Insurance
  • HOA Fees
  • PMI (if applicable)

Example: If your household earns $6,000 per month before taxes, your maximum housing payment should be $1,680 ($6,000 x 0.28).

2. The Back-End Ratio (36%)

Your total monthly debt payments (including your new mortgage) should not exceed **36% of your gross monthly income**. This includes your housing costs plus:

  • Car loans / leases
  • Student loans
  • Credit card minimum payments
  • Personal loans

Example: If you earn $6,000 a month, your total debt payments (housing + car + credit cards) should stay under $2,160.

Note: Some lenders allow a Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio up to 43% or even 50% for qualified buyers. However, just because a bank approves you for a higher amount doesn't mean you should take it. Sticking closer to the 28/36 rule leaves room for savings and emergencies.

Why Your Pre-Approval Amount Isn't Your True Budget

When you get pre-approved for a mortgage, the lender will tell you the maximum amount you can borrow. Many buyers assume this is their budget. This is a common mistake.

Lenders only look at numbers on a screen. They don't know that you want to save for retirement, travel, or start a family. A mortgage payment that a lender considers "safe" might make you feel "house poor" in real life.

Factors That Affect How Much You Can Afford

Beyond your income, several other factors play a big role in your home-buying budget:

1. Your Credit Score

A higher credit score gets you a lower interest rate. Even a 1% difference in your rate can save you tens of thousands of dollars over a 30-year loan, allowing you to afford a slightly more expensive home for the same monthly payment.

2. Your Down Payment

If you put down 20% or more, you avoid PMI, which lowers your monthly payment. You can use our calculator to see how different down payment amounts change your monthly costs.

3. Your Lifestyle and Future Goals

Ask yourself these questions:

  • Do you plan to have children soon?
  • Are you expecting a major change in income?
  • Do you want to travel frequently?
  • Do you have an emergency fund saved up?

Don't Forget the Hidden Costs of Homeownership

Your mortgage payment is just the beginning. When calculating what you can afford, leave room for these expenses:

  • Closing Costs: Expect to pay 2% to 5% of the purchase price upfront for lender fees, title insurance, and appraisals.
  • Maintenance and Repairs: A good rule of thumb is to save 1% of your home's value each year for maintenance.
  • Utilities: If you are moving from a small apartment to a large house, your heating, cooling, and water bills will be much higher.

Conclusion: How to Find Your Number

To find your true budget, start by calculating your monthly take-home pay. Then, work backward using the 28/36 rule. Use our free mortgage calculator to plug in different home prices, down payments, and interest rates until you find a monthly payment that feels comfortable and leaves room for your other financial goals.

A house is more than an investment; it is your home. Make sure the payment allows you to enjoy living in it without financial stress.
